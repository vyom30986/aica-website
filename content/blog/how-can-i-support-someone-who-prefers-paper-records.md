## Short answer

Build a clear paper system around the person's habits and add digital copies only where they are useful and agreed. A labelled physical folder can work well when its contents are kept current and easy to retrieve.

## Practical explanation

Use simple sections for appointments, reports and provider summaries. Keep pages in date order and avoid separating multipage documents. Add a front-page list of upcoming tasks if the person finds that helpful. If you scan records, explain where the copies will be stored and who can access them. Review the folder together after visits so new paperwork reaches the right section.

## Example

A folder with three familiar tabs may be easier to maintain than an elaborate filing system that only one family member understands.
