Most people, when they hear I'm 16 and building a healthcare app, ask the same question in slightly different ways. "Itni jaldi?" "Shouldn't you focus on studies?" "What do you know about healthcare?" They're fair questions. This is my honest answer.

I'm Vyom, and I'm building AICA in Lucknow. Here's why, and why I don't think I should wait.

## The problem is everywhere, once you notice it

You don't need to be a doctor to see how medicine works in an Indian household. A prescription on paper that only the chemist can read. A strip of tablets with no clear memory of whether the afternoon dose was taken. Reports scattered across WhatsApp chats. A son or daughter in another city trying to manage a parent's BP over the phone.

None of these are rare. They're normal. That's what bothers me. When something this important runs on memory, luck and forwarded photos, it's not really a system. It's people doing their best without the right tools.

## The three people who never talk

The more I looked at it, the clearer one thing became. There are three people involved in almost every prescription: the patient, the chemist and the doctor. And they barely share information with each other.

- The doctor writes the prescription and often doesn't know if it was filled or followed
- The chemist reads it, hands over the medicines and doesn't know when the patient will run out
- The patient, or their family, leaves with a paper slip and tries to remember everything

AICA is my attempt to put all three on one shared prescription thread. The patient scans the prescription, the AI reads it, and the person confirms it against the photo before anything is saved. From there, dose reminders, refills and the next clinic visit can all work from the same information.

## Why now, and not after college

There's a common idea that you should build things only after you've "become something." I understand the logic. But the problem isn't waiting for me to graduate. Families are dealing with it today.

Being young also has advantages I'd be foolish to waste. I have time to learn slowly, ask people questions without pretending to know the answers, and make mistakes while the stakes are small. I'd rather start now, carefully, than start later with more confidence and less curiosity.

## What I'm careful about

Healthcare isn't a place to move fast and break things. Being 16 makes me more careful, not less. A few rules I've set for AICA:

- The AI reads, but a human always confirms before anything is saved
- The app doesn't give medical advice. Doses come from the doctor, questions go to the doctor or pharmacist
- Data is handled with consent, and you can download or delete it. We're building with India's DPDP Act in mind
- The core patient app, with prescriptions, dose alarms, refills and family profiles, stays free forever

## Where things stand

I want to be honest about this too. AICA is early. There's an interactive prototype and a waitlist at aicaglobal.in. Pilots are starting in Lucknow with founding pharmacies and clinics, who use it free during the pilot. It isn't in app stores yet, and nothing is charged today.

That's the starting line, not the finish. I'd rather tell you exactly where we are than make it sound bigger than it is.

## The takeaway

I'm building AICA at 16 because the problem is real, it's happening in every family right now, and I'd rather start carefully today than wait for permission. If that sounds useful, join the waitlist at aicaglobal.in.
