It's 11 pm. Papa is brushing his teeth when he suddenly stops. "Arre, shaam wali goli toh li hi nahi." Now there's a small family debate at the bathroom door. Mummy says take it now. Your brother on the phone says take two tomorrow to make up. Papa says skip it, one day won't matter.

All three are guessing. Here's what you should actually do.

## First, don't panic

Missing a dose happens to almost everyone who takes regular medicines. It's common, it's human, and in many cases a single missed dose isn't dangerous. The goal is to handle it calmly and correctly, not to fix it in a rush.

What you do next depends on the specific medicine. There's no single rule that fits every tablet, and that's why guessing is the problem.

## The one rule that's almost universal

**Don't double up unless a doctor or pharmacist tells you to.**

Taking two doses together to "catch up" can mean taking more of the medicine than the body is meant to handle at once. For some medicines that's mostly harmless. For others, like certain BP, sugar or heart medicines, it can cause real problems.

So whatever the family debate says, taking an extra dose shouldn't be the default.

## Where to find the right answer

**Check the leaflet.** Many medicines come with a paper leaflet in the box. It often has a section on what to do if you miss a dose. It's small print, but it's written for exactly this moment.

**Ask the pharmacist.** Your local chemist can usually tell you quickly what the general advice is for that medicine. A quick call works.

**Call the doctor's clinic** if it's a medicine you've been told is critical, or if the pharmacist suggests it.

**Write down what you're told.** Next time, you won't need to ask again for the same medicine.

## Things to keep a note of

When you ask for advice, it helps to have these ready:

- The medicine name and strength, as written on the strip
- What time the dose was supposed to be taken
- What time it is now
- When the next dose is due
- Whether any other doses were missed recently

This lets the pharmacist or doctor give you a more useful answer.

## When a missed dose needs more attention

Some situations deserve a quicker call to the doctor:

- Missing several doses in a row
- Missing doses of a medicine the doctor has said is very important not to miss
- Feeling unwell after a missed dose

And if someone has chest pain, sudden weakness, confusion, trouble breathing or faints, don't wait to figure out the medicine question. Call 112 or get to a hospital immediately.

## Making misses less likely

Once the moment has passed, it's worth asking why the dose was missed. Usually it's one of a few things:

- **No clear routine.** Tie doses to fixed daily moments, like morning chai or bedtime.
- **Too many medicines.** A pill organiser with morning, afternoon and night slots makes it visible.
- **Out of stock.** The strip ran out. Track refill dates so this doesn't happen.
- **Away from home.** Carry a day's doses when going out, or travelling.
- **Unclear instructions.** If "1-0-1" or "BD" was confusing, ask the chemist to write it plainly.

A reminder alarm on the phone, labelled clearly with what to take, also helps a lot.

## The takeaway

Missed a dose? Don't double up unless you've been told to. Check the leaflet or ask the pharmacist, and note what they say for next time. Then fix the reason it was missed.
