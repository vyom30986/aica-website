It's 9:40 at night in Aliganj. A young man walks up to the counter, phone in hand, and shows the chemist uncle a screenshot. "Online pe yeh sasta mil raha hai." Uncle looks at the screen, looks at the strip in his hand, and sighs. Every neighbourhood chemist in India has lived this exact moment.

Online pharmacies are not going away. They have discounts, doorstep delivery and big budgets. But the small chemist has things no app can copy easily, and most shops are not using them on purpose. Here is how a corner shop can stay the first choice.

## Stop competing on the thing you will lose

A small shop cannot win a pure price war against a company that can afford to lose money for years. Trying to match every discount usually just eats the margin that keeps the lights on.

The better question is: what does the customer get here that they cannot get from a delivery box? Usually the answer is people, speed and memory.

## Speed is your real delivery

When Papa's BP medicine runs out on a Sunday evening, nobody wants to wait for a delivery slot. They want it in ten minutes. The local chemist is already the fastest delivery service in the area, it just doesn't call itself that.

A few simple ways to lean into it:

- Take orders on WhatsApp and keep them ready for pickup
- Offer home delivery within a short radius for regular customers, even if it is the shop boy on a scooter
- Keep the common chronic medicines of your regular patients always in stock

That last one matters most. If you know Mrs Srivastava needs her sugar tablets every month, running out is the one thing that sends her online.

## Be the person who explains

An online order arrives with a printed bill and nothing else. A chemist can say, "Yeh khaane ke baad lena, aur yeh wala subah." For an elderly patient, or a son buying for his mother, that thirty seconds of explanation is worth more than a small discount.

This does not mean giving medical advice. It means repeating what the doctor wrote, clearly, and saying "doctor se ek baar confirm kar lijiye" when something looks unusual. Customers remember the counter where someone actually looked at them.

## Remember people, not just stock

Big platforms remember purchase history because software does it for them. Small chemists remember faces, which is better, but it lives only in uncle's head. When he is on leave, the memory goes with him.

Writing it down, even simply, changes things:

- Who comes for which regular medicines
- Roughly when they last bought a month's supply
- Their phone number, with their permission, for a refill reminder

A short message like "Aapki medicine khatam hone wali hogi, ready rakh dein?" brings people back without any discount at all.

## Make trust visible

People trust the corner shop because they can walk in and talk to someone. Show it. Keep the counter clean, store medicines properly, give proper bills, and check expiry dates in front of the customer when it makes sense.

When someone asks for a substitute because the prescribed one is out of stock, be honest about it and suggest they check with the doctor if there's any doubt. That honesty is a big reason people keep coming back instead of trusting a stranger's warehouse.

## Use a little technology, on your terms

Going digital doesn't mean building an app. It can be as small as a WhatsApp business number, a register of regular patients, or a tool that reads prescriptions and reminds you when a refill is due. The goal is to make your existing strengths easier to keep up, not to become an online pharmacy yourself.

## The takeaway

You won't win on discounts, and you don't need to. Win on being close, being quick, and remembering people. A screenshot can show a price. It cannot say "aaiye uncle, aapki dawai ready hai."
