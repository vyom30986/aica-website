You hand the folded slip across the counter. The chemist glances at it, turns to the shelves, pulls out three strips, and hands the paper back. Or maybe he keeps it. Or maybe he takes a photo. You pay, say thank you, and leave. Two weeks later, at the doctor's, someone asks, "Purana prescription hai?" and you realise you have no idea where it went.

That small slip of paper does a lot of travelling. Here's what usually happens to it.

## Step one: the reading

The first thing a pharmacist does is read it. That sounds simple until you've seen a busy doctor's handwriting at the end of a long OPD.

The chemist works out:

- Which medicines are prescribed
- The strength of each
- The dose pattern, like 1-0-1 or BD
- How long to take each one
- Any special notes, like before or after food

If something is unclear, a careful chemist will ask you about it, or call the clinic. That's a good sign, not an inconvenience.

## Step two: the matching

Next, the chemist matches each line to what's on the shelf. Sometimes the exact product isn't in stock, and they may suggest another version with the same ingredient and strength.

If that happens, it's worth asking a few questions. Is it the same active ingredient? The same strength and form? If you're not sure, ask the pharmacist to confirm, or check with the doctor. And make sure the person taking it knows the box will look different.

## Step three: what happens to the paper

This part varies a lot from store to store, and depends on the type of medicine:

- **Returned to you.** The most common outcome for many regular medicines. You keep the original
- **Kept or copied.** For some kinds of medicines, a pharmacy may need to retain the prescription or a copy as part of their records
- **Photographed.** Some stores take a quick photo for their own reference
- **Written on.** Some chemists mark the prescription to note what was supplied

If you're not sure what's happened to yours, just ask before you leave the counter.

## Step four: the slow disappearance

Once the paper comes home, its life gets uncertain. It goes into a drawer, a plastic file, a handbag, the back of a calendar. It gets folded, wet, faded. Sometimes it ends up as a shopping list.

Then, months later, someone needs it:

- The doctor wants to know what was prescribed last time
- A new doctor asks for the current medicine list
- A refill needs a valid prescription
- A family member in another city wants to check the dose

And the slip is nowhere.

## How to keep track of it

A few easy habits:

- **Take a clear photo the moment you get it,** before handing it over. Good light, flat surface, full page in frame
- **Keep the original in one place.** A single folder for each family member works well
- **Write the date and doctor's name** on the back if it isn't clear
- **Keep the latest one on top,** and don't throw away old ones until you've checked with the doctor
- **Share the photo with whoever handles refills,** so they're not guessing

And if the chemist ever gives you something that doesn't match what you expected, ask before taking it. If something feels badly wrong after a new medicine, like trouble breathing, swelling of the face, chest pain or fainting, get urgent help or call 112.

## The takeaway

A paper prescription gets read, matched, maybe kept, and then too often lost. Photograph it before you hand it over, keep the original in one folder, and ask the chemist what they're doing with it.
