You install a reminder app on Mummy's phone, set everything up, and proudly show her. She nods. Two days later you call and ask if she's been using it. "Woh beep beep wala? Maine band kar diya, bahut shor karta tha."

Getting parents to use any new app is hard. A health app is harder, because it touches something personal and it arrives with the faint suggestion that they can't manage on their own. The good news is that a bit of patience and the right approach go a long way.

## Start with their problem, not your app

"Maine ek app daali hai" sounds like homework. "Aapko yaad rakhne ki tension nahi rahegi ki raat wali goli li ya nahi" sounds like help.

Find the one thing that actually bothers them. Maybe it's forgetting the afternoon dose, or losing old prescriptions, or not knowing when to refill. Introduce the app as the answer to that one thing, and nothing else at first.

## Do it together, in person if you can

Setting it up remotely and handing it over rarely works. Sit with them, ideally on a calm day, not right before guests arrive.

- Let them hold the phone and tap the buttons themselves
- Go slowly and repeat steps without sighing
- Increase the font size and volume in the phone settings first
- Write the two or three most important steps on paper and stick it near where they keep their medicines

If you're in another city, a video call works, but keep it short. Two small sessions beat one long, frustrating one.

## Keep it to one feature first

Most apps do many things. Your parent needs one, at the beginning. If it's dose reminders, just set up reminders. Don't add vitals, diary entries and family sharing in the same afternoon.

Once the reminder becomes part of their day, add the next thing. Habits stack better than features.

## Respect their pride and their privacy

Parents managed their lives, and yours, for decades. Being told how to take their own medicine can sting. A few things help:

- Ask, don't announce. "Kya main bhi dekh sakta hoon ki dawai li ya nahi?" is better than secretly checking
- Make it clear they're in control and can see everything you can see
- Don't use the app to scold them when a dose is missed

The app should feel like a helper for them, not a monitoring tool for you.

## Expect a few rough weeks

There will be confusion. The reminder will go off during a puja, or they'll mute it by accident, or a phone update will change something. That's normal. Check in gently, fix small issues quickly, and don't give up after the first week.

Also, an app doesn't replace the doctor or pharmacist. If a parent has a question about a dose or wants to change anything, the answer comes from them, not from the phone.

## Choose something built for them

Look for an app with large, clear text, simple words, support for how Indian prescriptions are written (like 1-0-1 or half a tablet), and a way for family to help without taking over. If your parent shares a phone or isn't comfortable with phones at all, an app where you can manage their profile from your own phone may work better.

## The takeaway

Lead with their problem, set it up together, start with one feature, and respect that it's their health. Most parents come around once the app proves it's on their side.
