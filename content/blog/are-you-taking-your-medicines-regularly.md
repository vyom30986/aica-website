"Medicines regularly le rahe ho?" Mummy nods. "Haan, le li." Then she glances at you, and you both know that's only mostly true. There were the three days at Mausi's house. And the week the strip ran out and nobody noticed till Sunday.

Every doctor asks this question, often more than once. It can feel like a formality or even an accusation. It's neither. It's one of the most important questions in the whole consultation.

## Why the doctor needs the honest answer

When a doctor sees that BP or sugar isn't where it should be, there are two very different possibilities. Either the medicine isn't working well enough for this person, or the medicine isn't being taken the way it was prescribed.

These two situations need completely different responses. If the doctor assumes the medicine was taken properly when it wasn't, they might increase the dose or add another drug, when the real fix was just taking the original one on time. That's why "haan" when the true answer is "mostly" can lead the plan in the wrong direction.

## Why people miss medicines

Almost nobody skips medicines on purpose to be difficult. Life gets in the way. Some very ordinary reasons:

- Forgetting, especially with afternoon doses or when the routine changes
- Confusion about which tablet is which when there are several
- The strip ran out and the refill was delayed
- Feeling better and assuming it's no longer needed
- Side effects that felt uncomfortable
- Cost, or the medicine not being available at the usual shop
- Travel, festivals, weddings, guests at home

All of these are worth telling the doctor. Each one has a different solution.

## Saying it without guilt

The goal isn't a perfect score. It's an accurate picture. You can say something as simple as:

- "Morning wali theek se leti hain, raat wali kabhi kabhi miss ho jaati hai"
- "Last month about a week missed hua, strip khatam ho gaya tha"
- "Yeh wali lene se pet kharab hota hai, isliye kam li"

Doctors hear this every day. A clear, honest answer helps them help you. And if a medicine is causing trouble, the doctor or pharmacist can suggest what to do. Please don't stop or change it on your own.

## Making regular easier

Small habits do more than willpower:

- Tie each dose to something that already happens daily, like morning chai or dinner
- Use a weekly pill box, filled on the same day every week
- Keep a simple tick sheet on the fridge, or use phone reminders
- Note the date the strip will run out and refill a few days early
- When travelling, pack medicines first, not last

For families managing a parent from far away, a quick daily check-in call at dose time can help too, as long as it feels like care and not policing.

## Bring the proof

If you keep any kind of record, a tick sheet, reminder history or a diary, bring it to the visit. It turns "I think she takes them" into something the doctor can actually use.

## The takeaway

When the doctor asks if you're taking your medicines regularly, the honest answer is the useful one. Missed doses are common and fixable, but only if the doctor knows about them.
