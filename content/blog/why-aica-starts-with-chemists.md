When I explain AICA, people often expect me to say we're starting with doctors. Doctors write the prescriptions, after all. Hospitals have the systems and the budgets. So when I say we're starting with the chemist shop on the corner, the reaction is usually a raised eyebrow. "Chemist? Kyun?"

Here's my reasoning.

## The chemist sees the patient more often than anyone

Think about someone on long-term medicines for BP, sugar or thyroid. They might see their doctor a few times a year. But they visit the chemist every month, sometimes more. The chemist knows their face, their name, often their whole family.

In a lot of Indian neighbourhoods, the chemist is the most regular healthcare contact a family has. If you want to help people keep up with their medicines, the counter where they actually pick them up is the natural place to begin.

## The chemist already reads the prescription

Every prescription in India passes through a chemist's hands. They are the ones who decode the handwriting, match it to the right medicine and explain the timings. That's exactly the moment where information can either get captured properly or get lost.

AICA helps here directly. The AI reads the doctor's handwriting, and a person confirms it against the photo before it's saved. For a busy counter, that's a practical helper, not a replacement for the chemist's judgement.

## The chemist has a clear, everyday problem

Small chemists face real pressure. Online pharmacies, discount apps, customers who quietly stop coming back. Most shops don't have a simple way to know when a regular patient is about to run out, or to remind them.

That's a problem AICA can help with from day one:

- Refill predictions, so the shop knows who is likely due soon
- Bringing patients back to the same counter instead of losing them
- Less guesswork at the counter when reading prescriptions

When a tool solves an obvious daily problem, people are willing to try it. That matters a lot for something this early.

## Good for the patient too

This isn't only about the shop. When a chemist reminds a patient before the strip runs out, the patient is less likely to miss doses. When the prescription is captured clearly at the counter, the patient's family can see the same information on their phone, with reminders set in Indian patterns like 1-0-1.

The chemist's interest and the patient's interest point in the same direction. That's rare, and it's a good place to build.

## Doctors are part of it, just not the first step

None of this means doctors are less important. AICA links patients, chemists and doctors on one shared prescription thread, and clinics get a patient summary and intake before the visit. But doctors are busy, clinics vary a lot in how they work, and changing their routine takes time.

Starting with chemists lets us build the shared thread at the point where prescriptions already flow every day. As that thread becomes useful, bringing the clinic in becomes a much easier conversation.

## Starting local

We're beginning with pilots in Lucknow. Founding pharmacies and clinics use AICA free during the pilot, and nothing is charged today. It's an early stage, with an interactive prototype and a waitlist at aicaglobal.in. I'd rather get it right with a few counters in one city than spread thin across many.

## The takeaway

AICA starts with chemists because that's where patients show up most often, where every prescription passes through, and where a clear daily problem is waiting to be solved. If you run a pharmacy in Lucknow, I'd love to hear from you at aicaglobal.in.
