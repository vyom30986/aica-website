"Free hai? Phir paisa kahan se aayega?" It's the first question almost everyone asks when I say the core AICA app is free forever. It's a good question. Free things on the internet often come with a catch, and with health data the catch can be serious.

So here's exactly what's free, why, and how we plan to keep it that way.

## What "core" means

The core patient app is the part that helps you and your family take medicines correctly. That includes:

- Scanning prescriptions, with AI reading them and you confirming against the photo
- Dose reminders, including Indian patterns like 1-0-1 and half tablets
- Refills, so you know before a strip runs out
- Family profiles, so you can manage your parents and others from one phone

All of this is free forever. Not a free trial, not free for the first month. Free.

## Why these features can't sit behind a paywall

The core is about not missing medicines. If someone can't afford a subscription, they still need their BP tablet on time. Putting reminders or refills behind a payment would mean the people who need help most get the least of it.

There's a family reason too. Often the person using AICA is a son or daughter managing several people: Mummy, Papa, maybe a grandparent. Charging per person just to remember medicines would punish exactly the families who are already doing the most care.

## How AICA plans to sustain itself

Free for patients doesn't mean free forever for everything. The plan has a few parts:

- **AICA Pro** is planned at ₹600 per month per profile, for people who want extra features beyond the core. The core stays free whether or not you ever upgrade
- **Pharmacies and clinics** get tools built for their side of the counter, like reading prescriptions, refill predictions and patient summaries before a visit. Founding pharmacies and clinics use AICA free during the pilot, and post-pilot pricing will be shared with them well in advance. We're early, with an interactive prototype, a waitlist at aicaglobal.in and pilots starting in Lucknow.

## What free does not mean

This matters most to me. A free health app should never pay for itself by quietly using your data in ways you didn't agree to.

So AICA is built around a few clear rules:

- Consent at sign-up, so you know what you're agreeing to
- You can download your data
- You can delete your data
- Built with India's DPDP Act in mind

If the price of a free app is your family's health information, it isn't free. I don't want AICA to work that way.

## Why I'm comfortable with this choice

Some people say it's risky to promise "free forever" so early. Maybe. But I'd rather make the promise now, when it shapes how everything else is designed, than try to add it later. Building the paid parts around a free core forces me to make the paid parts genuinely worth paying for.

And honestly, the core features are the reason AICA exists. If they don't reach as many families as possible, the rest doesn't matter much.

## The takeaway

The core AICA app, prescriptions, dose alarms, refills and family profiles, stays free forever because remembering medicines shouldn't depend on a subscription. AICA Pro is how we plan to sustain it, and your data is never the price. Join the waitlist at aicaglobal.in.
